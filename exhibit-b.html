Influencer Engagement Agreement — Contract Review

Exhibit B · Contract Review · Issue-Spotting Exercise

Parties: GlowNest Beauty Pvt. Ltd. / Ms. Riya Malhotra (fictional)
Context: Completed as part of LawSikho's Contract Drafting Bootcamp. A deliberately flawed Influencer Engagement Agreement was provided for review; the exercise required identifying drafting errors, one-sided or ambiguous terms, and legal risks, and proposing corrected language.

ISSUE 1 — PAYMENT TRIGGER

Original clause: "...payable upon successful completion of the campaign and subject to the Brand's satisfaction with the deliverables." (Cl. 3.1)

Issue identified: "Subject to the Brand's satisfaction" is a subjective, undefined standard. As drafted, the Brand could withhold payment indefinitely simply by claiming dissatisfaction, with no objective benchmark the Influencer could point to.

Corrected clause: Fixed fee tied to completion of Deliverables per Schedule A, with a defined Net-30 payment window from invoice date, and a 12% p.a. late-payment interest penalty for delays beyond that window. (Cl. 4.1–4.2)

ISSUE 2 — INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY ASSIGNMENT

Original clause: "All content created by the Influencer... shall be owned by the Brand. The Influencer hereby assigns all rights in such content to the Brand." (Cl. 4.1)

Issue identified: Unconditional, unqualified assignment of all rights, with no carve-out allowing the Influencer to use the content in her own portfolio or showreel.

Corrected clause: Assignment made conditional on receipt of full payment, and the Brand grants back a perpetual, royalty-free license permitting the Influencer to display the content on her own channels and portfolio for self-promotional and archival purposes. (Cl. 5.1–5.2)

ISSUE 3 — EXCLUSIVITY / RESTRAINT OF TRADE

Original clause: "...the Influencer shall not promote any beauty, cosmetics, or personal care brands. This obligation shall survive termination of this Agreement." (Cl. 5.1)

Issue identified: The restriction is not limited to competitors, and it survives termination with no end date, amounting to an indefinite restraint of trade — generally unenforceable under Section 27, Indian Contract Act, 1872.

Corrected clause: Restriction narrowed to a defined list of direct competitors (Schedule B), applies only during the active Term, and expressly dissolves on termination or expiry. (Cl. 6.1–6.2)

ISSUE 4 — TERMINATION (ONE-SIDED)

Original clause: "The Brand may terminate this Agreement at any time by providing 7 (seven) days' written notice to the Influencer for any reason whatsoever." (Cl. 8.1)

Issue identified: Only the Brand holds a termination-for-convenience right; the Influencer has none, and no compensation mechanism exists if the Brand terminates mid-campaign.

Corrected clause: Mutual termination-for-convenience right (15 days' notice, either Party), plus a Kill-Fee provision: if the Brand terminates after work has commenced but before publication, it must pay 50% of the total fee or the pro-rata value of completed milestones, whichever is higher. (Cl. 9.1–9.2)

ISSUE 5 — SUBJECTIVE "SOLE OPINION" TERMINATION

Original clause: "The Brand may terminate this Agreement with immediate effect if, in its sole opinion, the Influencer's conduct... bring[s]... the Brand into disrepute." (Cl. 8.2)

Issue identified: "In its sole opinion" gives the Brand unchecked, subjective discretion, effectively an at-will termination dressed up as a morality clause.

Corrected clause: Reframed around an objective standard: material breach must remain uncured for 7 days before termination for cause; the morality-linked termination is limited to conduct causing "widespread public disrepute," and payment is expressly preserved for content already published. (Cl. 9.3)

ISSUE 6 — INDEMNITY SCOPE

Original clause: "The Influencer agrees to indemnify... the Brand harmless from any and all claims, damages, losses... arising out of or in connection with the Influencer's performance... or any third-party claims arising from the content." (Cl. 9.1)

Issue identified: Broad enough to capture claims that have nothing to do with the Influencer's fault, e.g. a product-liability claim arising from a genuine defect in the Brand's own product.

Corrected clause: Indemnity narrowed to losses arising from her own breach of IP warranties or advertising-law violations, with an express carve-out excluding claims arising from the Brand's product defects or written instructions. (Cl. 10.1–10.2)

ISSUE 7 — LOPSIDED LIABILITY CAP

Original clause: "...the Brand's total liability under this Agreement shall not exceed INR 50,000." (Cl. 10.1)

Issue identified: Only the Brand's liability is capped, at a token amount, while the Influencer's liability under the broad indemnity clause is left completely uncapped.

Corrected clause: Mutual liability cap for both Parties, set at the full contract value (INR 4,50,000), with a carve-out for gross negligence or wilful misconduct. (Cl. 11.1)

ISSUE 8 — EVERGREEN AUTO-RENEWAL

Original clause: "This Agreement shall be valid for a period of three (3) months... and shall stand renewed for further periods unless terminated in accordance with the provisions hereof." (Cl. 2.1)

Issue identified: Automatic evergreen renewal with no opt-in step could trap the Influencer in successive renewal periods without a fresh negotiation on fees or scope.

Corrected clause: Fixed 3-month Term with automatic expiry (no evergreen renewal); any extension requires mutual written consent executed at least 15 days before the Term ends. (Cl. 3.1–3.2)

ISSUE 9 — ONE-SIDED CONFIDENTIALITY

Original clause: "The Influencer shall keep all information shared by the Brand confidential and shall not disclose the same to any third party." (Cl. 6.1)

Issue identified: Confidentiality obligations run only one way, with no standard carve-outs for publicly available information or legally compelled disclosure.

Corrected clause: Confidentiality obligation made mutual, and standard exceptions added for information that becomes public through no fault of the receiving Party, or that must be disclosed under a court or government order (with prompt notice to allow a protective order to be sought). (Cl. 8.1–8.2)
