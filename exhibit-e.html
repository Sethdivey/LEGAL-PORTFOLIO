Corporate Due Diligence — Mock M&A Due Diligence Report

Exhibit E · Corporate / M&A Due Diligence

Transaction: Fictional acquisition of ABC by XYZ
Purpose: Portfolio exhibit demonstrating issue spotting, legal-risk assessment, documentary due diligence, and buyer-side transaction protections
Framework used: Red Flag / Finding → Legal Issue → Risk → DD Checks → Buyer Action
Note: Fictional, educational exhibit — not legal advice and not a report on a real transaction

SITUATION 1 — EMPLOYEE TRUST SHARES / CAP TABLE MEDIUM

Red Flag / Finding: 50,000 shares were held through an employee trust and were not adequately explained. Shareholder arrangements included ROFR, tag-along and drag-along rights, but treatment of the employee-trust shares was unclear.

Legal Issue: Whether the employee-trust shares can be acquired as part of a 100% acquisition, and whether trust documents, employee rights, or consents restrict transfer.

Risk: XYZ may be unable to obtain 100% ownership or may face disputes concerning the shares.

DD Checks: Review trust documents, allotment records, trust terms, shareholder arrangements, transfer restrictions, and required consents.

Buyer Action: Obtain documentary confirmation of transferability and, where necessary, make transfer/settlement of the shares a Condition Precedent with appropriate seller protection.

SITUATION 2 — BANK CHARGE / OUTSTANDING WORKING CAPITAL FACILITY HIGH

Red Flag / Finding: ABC had a ₹6 crore working-capital facility secured by a charge in favour of a bank. The seller said it was substantially repaid, but current information indicated a small amount remained outstanding.

Legal Issue: Whether the facility and security remained active, and what liability and secured asset were involved.

Risk: XYZ could acquire ABC while a bank liability and security interest remain in place.

DD Checks: Verify facility documents, outstanding balance, repayment history, secured asset, charge records, bank consent/NOC, and release procedure.

Buyer Action: Require repayment, release of the charge, and documentary bank NOC as a Condition Precedent before closing.

SITUATION 3 — CORE SOFTWARE / EMPLOYEE IP OWNERSHIP HIGH

Red Flag / Finding: ABC's core software was developed by employees, including a significant former employee, but no separate IP assignment documentation was provided and employee contractual wording was not uniform.

Legal Issue: Whether ABC owns the software and related IP in full, or whether rights remain with current/former employees.

Risk: XYZ could acquire ABC without complete ownership or control of business-critical technology.

DD Checks: Review employment agreements, IP assignment provisions, creation records, source-code ownership records, and former-employee assignments.

Buyer Action: Require documentary evidence of ownership and assignment/settlement of outstanding employee IP rights before closing where necessary.

SITUATION 4 — REGULATORY LICENCE RENEWAL / UNRESOLVED COMPLIANCE MEDIUM

Red Flag / Finding: A material regulatory licence was due for renewal four months after closing. Six months earlier, the regulator had raised a compliance issue, and no final closure evidence was found.

Legal Issue: Whether the compliance matter is resolved, and whether it could affect renewal, delay, penalties, or other regulatory action.

Risk: Renewal could be delayed, conditioned, or penalised, potentially disrupting ABC's business.

DD Checks: Review licence, renewal requirements, regulator correspondence, ABC's response, remediation/closure evidence, and possible consequences.

Buyer Action: Obtain documentary closure evidence; where appropriate, make satisfactory resolution/renewal a Condition Precedent and seek indemnity for identified pre-closing losses.

SITUATION 5 — MAJOR CUSTOMER / CHANGE-OF-CONTROL CONSENT HIGH

Red Flag / Finding: Omega generated approximately ₹8 crore of annual revenue and its contract contained a change-of-control provision allowing termination. No formal consent was found, and Omega had recently requested details about the transition.

Legal Issue: Whether the acquisition triggers Omega's termination right, and whether prior consent/notice is required.

Risk: XYZ could lose a highly material customer after closing.

DD Checks: Review the contract, change-of-control clause, notice requirements, correspondence, and any formal consent, waiver, or amendment.

Buyer Action: Require formal written consent/confirmation from Omega before closing; if unavailable, negotiate specific indemnity or other transaction protection.

SITUATION 6 — KEY SUPPLIER / CHANGE-OF-CONTROL RISK HIGH

Red Flag / Finding: Titan supplied approximately 35% of ABC's raw materials. The material was not readily available, and Titan's contract allowed termination on a change of control with 30 days' prior notice. Management said Titan would continue, but evidence was informal.

Legal Issue: Whether Titan can terminate following acquisition, and whether consent is required.

Risk: Loss of Titan could materially disrupt production because of supplier dependence and lack of an immediate alternative.

DD Checks: Review supplier agreement, termination/change-of-control provisions, notices, correspondence, alternatives, and production impact.

Buyer Action: Obtain formal written consent from Titan confirming continuation, preferably as a Condition Precedent where justified.

SITUATION 7 — DISTRIBUTOR EXCLUSIVITY / RENEWAL COMMITMENT MEDIUM

Red Flag / Finding: Nova distributed approximately 20% of ABC's products in a region and had exclusive rights. The contract had 18 months remaining, a ₹6 crore annual minimum commitment, and automatic two-year renewal unless 90 days' prior notice of non-renewal was given. ABC had considered replacing Nova for poor performance.

Legal Issue: The 90-day provision concerns non-renewal and should not automatically be treated as an early-termination right. Actual early-termination provisions must be reviewed.

Risk: ABC may remain tied to Nova for the remaining term and potentially another two years, with the minimum commitment.

DD Checks: Review the agreement, early-termination rights, performance breaches, minimum commitment history, and renewal dates.

Buyer Action: Establish actual termination/replacement rights and, if necessary, negotiate consent, a Condition Precedent, indemnity, or purchase-price protection.

SITUATION 8 — PENDING ₹4 CRORE LITIGATION CLAIM MEDIUM

Red Flag / Finding: Gamma Industries filed a civil suit claiming approximately ₹4 crore for alleged contractual losses. The case had been pending 14 months, and external counsel stated that the claim could not presently be ruled out.

Legal Issue: ABC has a potentially material contingent liability that may survive closing. The contractual indemnity and merits require assessment.

Risk: XYZ may bear post-closing economic consequences, including damages/settlement and litigation costs. ₹4 crore is a claim amount, not an established liability.

DD Checks: Review agreement, pleadings, court orders, legal advice, alleged breach, evidence, quantum, settlement discussions, costs, and financial-statement provisions/disclosures.

Buyer Action: Assess and quantify exposure; consider settlement before closing; otherwise negotiate specific seller indemnity and potentially escrow/holdback or purchase-price protection.

SITUATION 9 — CRITICAL TECHNOLOGY CONTRACT / ANTI-ASSIGNMENT HIGH

Red Flag / Finding: Orion supplied a technology platform essential to ABC's daily operations. Two years remained and the annual cost was approximately ₹1.5 crore. The agreement contained an anti-assignment restriction, but no documented Orion consent, waiver, or amendment concerning the acquisition was found.

Legal Issue: Whether the acquisition triggers any contractual consequence, and whether Orion's consent/waiver is required. A change of control and assignment should not automatically be treated as identical without analysing the contract and applicable principles.

Risk: XYZ could acquire ABC without certainty that a business-critical platform can continue, creating operational disruption and replacement cost.

DD Checks: Review agreement, anti-assignment language, change-of-control wording, amendments, waivers, correspondence, and alternative technology options.

Buyer Action: If consent/waiver is required or commercially necessary, obtain formal written confirmation from Orion before closing and, where appropriate, make it a Condition Precedent.

SITUATION 10 — KEY EMPLOYEE / RETENTION RISK MEDIUM

Red Flag / Finding: Rahul Mehta, ABC's Head of Technology, had been with ABC for eight years and was described as essential to continued operations. He had a three-month notice period but no acquisition-related retention arrangement. He had access to source code, technical architecture, vendor relationships, and confidential information.

Legal Issue: Whether ABC has sufficient contractual and practical protections for key-person continuity and protection of confidential/technical assets after closing.

Risk: Rahul's departure could disrupt operations, cause loss of critical knowledge, and create confidentiality/IP risk.

DD Checks: Review employment agreement, confidentiality/IP provisions, notice and termination terms, return-of-property obligations, incentives, succession/knowledge-transfer plans, and correspondence about his future role.

Buyer Action: Where appropriate, negotiate a retention or updated employment arrangement with confidentiality, IP, and knowledge-transfer protections. If genuinely material, consider making the agreed arrangement a Condition Precedent.

PORTFOLIO NOTE

This is a fictional educational portfolio exhibit for the XYZ–ABC acquisition. It demonstrates the ability to identify DD red flags, distinguish contractual from commercial risk, identify documentary evidence required, and convert unresolved risk into appropriate buyer-side protections.

Not legal advice and not a report on a real transaction. Materiality tags (High/Medium) reflect the author's own risk-ranking judgment, added to illustrate prioritization within a DD report.
